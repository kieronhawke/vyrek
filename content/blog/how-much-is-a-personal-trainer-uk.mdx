Personal training pricing in the UK is strangely difficult to research, and I think I know why. The pages that rank for it are mostly written for trainers deciding what to charge, not for people deciding what to pay. So you end up reading advice aimed at somebody on the other side of the transaction.

Here is the buyer's version.

## What it costs

Broadly, UK personal training sits in a band from the mid-twenties to around sixty pounds an hour, and London sits above that, often considerably. Where you land inside that band depends on three things: where you are, whether the trainer works for a gym or for themselves, and how experienced they are.

The number that catches people out is not the hourly rate. It is the monthly total.

## The arithmetic nobody does before signing up

There are not four weeks in a month. There are about 4.33. That sounds pedantic until you see what it does to a budget over a year.

Two sessions a week at forty-five pounds is not three hundred and sixty pounds a month. It is a little over three hundred and eighty, and across a year it is more than four and a half thousand pounds.

That is not an argument against paying it. Plenty of people get four and a half thousand pounds of value out of it. It is an argument for knowing the number before the third direct debit rather than after.

## When a local trainer is the right call

I want to be genuinely useful here rather than just steering you toward what I sell. There are three situations where paying for in-person sessions is clearly the better decision, and I would tell anyone the same.

**You are learning to lift and you have nobody to check you.** Video review is good. It is not as good as someone standing next to you moving your elbow. If you are new to barbell work, a block of in-person sessions is money well spent, and it is a block, six or ten sessions to learn the patterns, not a permanent arrangement.

**You are coming back from an injury and you are nervous.** Having a person in the room who can tell you to stop is worth paying for. This is also a temporary phase.

**You genuinely will not go otherwise.** If the appointment in the diary and the money already spent is the only thing that gets you through the door, then that is what you are buying, and it is a legitimate purchase. Just be honest that this is what it is, because it changes what you should look for in a trainer.

## When online coaching does more for the money

The honest case for the thing I do.

With sessions, you pay per hour and the hours you buy are the hours you are coached. Two sessions a week is two coached hours out of however many you train. The rest of the week is on you.

With coaching, every session in the week is planned, whether you train twice or five times, and it adapts based on what you actually did. For the same money as two sessions a week you generally get all of your training programmed rather than a fraction of it.

That trade is a clear win if your problem is not knowing what to do. It is a clear loss if your problem is technique that needs a pair of eyes on it, or if you know precisely what you should be doing and simply will not do it without an appointment.

## Questions worth asking before you pay anyone

This applies whether you are hiring locally or online. If a coach is annoyed by these questions, that is your answer.

That last one matters more than it looks. A coach who has an answer to it is thinking about your outcome. A coach who has never considered it is thinking about your direct debit.
