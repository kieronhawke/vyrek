**Twelve weeks** if you can already run 5km without stopping and train in a gym. **Sixteen to twenty weeks** if you are strong but do not run. **Around six months** if you are starting from very little.

The variable that decides which of those you are is your running, not your strength. That surprises people, and it is the most useful thing on this page.

## Why trust this

The demands are set by the format itself: 8km of running split into eight 1km efforts, each one off a different kind of fatigue, per the official 26/27 rulebook. Ben Sutherland races Pro Doubles on the Elite 15 start list. His own first HYROX, in Berlin in 2024, took 1:01:51.

## Find your starting point

| You can... | Allow | The limiting factor |
|---|---|---|
| Run 5km comfortably, train in a gym | 12 weeks | Station familiarity and pacing |
| Lift well but rarely run | 16-20 weeks | Aerobic base. This is the big one |
| Run well but never lift | 12-14 weeks | Sled and lunge tolerance |
| Exercise occasionally, neither seriously | 20-24 weeks | Everything, but start with running |
| Very little activity currently | ~6 months | Consistency before intensity |

Be honest with the first column. "I used to run" is not the same as "I run".

## Why running decides it

The stations are heavy but short. The running is 8km, and it is the part that most reliably predicts finish time.

Aerobic fitness also improves on a slower timeline than strength does. You can meaningfully improve a sled push in six weeks. Building the engine that lets you run kilometre six at a reasonable pace takes months, and there is no shortcut, because the adaptations are structural rather than neural.

So if you have to choose where the extra weeks go, they go into running.

## What a twelve-week block actually looks like

Broadly, in three phases.

**Weeks 1-4, base.** Three runs a week: one easy, one interval session, one longer easy run. Two full-body strength sessions. No HYROX-specific work yet, and that is deliberate.

**Weeks 5-8, specific.** Same skeleton, but one session a week becomes compromised work: straight from a station into a run. This is the sensation the race is made of and it cannot be trained by running alone.

**Weeks 9-11, race weight.** Everything at your actual division loads. Weigh your gym's sled first, because rulebook figures **include** the sled. One half-simulation. One full simulation four to six weeks out, not closer.

**Week 12, taper.** Cut volume by roughly half, keep some intensity. You cannot gain fitness in the last week and you can lose freshness.

## When eight weeks is enough, and when it is not

Eight weeks works if you already have the base and just need to learn the event. You will finish, and you will learn the pacing lesson the hard way at station two.

Eight weeks does not work if you are building aerobic fitness from scratch. You will arrive undertrained, the back half will be miserable, and the most likely outcome is that you do not enter a second race. That is the real cost of rushing it.

If you have already entered a race that is eight weeks away and you are not ready, the honest options are to enter a later event, or to enter this one with finishing as the only goal and no time expectation. Both are fine. Pretending the timeline is longer than it is, is not.

## The practical part: choosing your date

1. **Count back from the race, not forward from today.** Put the block start in your calendar, not just the race date.
2. **Check what season the block falls in.** A December race means training through autumn; a March race in Scotland means training through winter. Seasonal disruption derails more blocks than fitness does.
3. **Add two weeks of slack.** Illness, work and life will take some. A sixteen-week plan that absorbs a bad fortnight beats a twelve-week plan that collapses.
4. **Book the second race before the first.** The most common regret is finishing, feeling great, and having nothing in the diary.
5. **Start the running now, regardless.** Whatever your block length, running base built before week one is never wasted.

## Honest limitations

These are ranges, not promises. People vary enormously in how fast they adapt, and age, training history, sleep and stress all move the number.

This page also assumes you are healthy. If you are returning from injury or have a medical condition, the timeline is a conversation with a professional, not a table on a blog.

And a longer block is not automatically better. Twenty weeks of half-hearted training beats twelve, but twelve weeks of consistent training beats twenty weeks of starting and stopping. Consistency is the actual variable.
