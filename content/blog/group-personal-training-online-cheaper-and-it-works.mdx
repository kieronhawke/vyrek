Online group coaching is a shared programme delivered remotely, with individual adjustment and regular check-ins. It costs less than one-to-one because the coach's time is split across several people rather than one.

For most people chasing general fitness or an endurance goal, it is the format that fits their actual constraints. It is also the format most easily faked, so the second half of this page is how to tell the difference.

## What it actually includes

A genuine online group programme has four things:

**A programme built for the group's goal**, with adjustment for individual histories, injuries and available equipment.

**Regular check-ins.** Weekly or fortnightly, where you report what you did and how it felt, and the plan changes in response.

**A way to ask questions** and get an answer from the coach rather than from the group.

**Someone who notices when you disappear.** This is the part that does the most work and the part cheapest to leave out.

If any of those is missing, what you have bought is a training plan with a chat attached.

## Who it suits

**People whose problem is knowing what to do**, not turning up. If you will train once someone tells you what to do, remote coaching solves your problem at a fraction of the cost of supervision.

**People with awkward schedules.** Shift work, small children, unpredictable weeks. A remote programme bends; a Tuesday 6pm slot does not.

**People training for a specific event** where the programming matters more than the supervision.

**People who have trained before** and need structure rather than teaching.

## Where it genuinely fails

Being honest about this matters more than selling it.

**If you will not train without someone there.** Remote accountability is real but it is not the same as a person waiting for you. If you know this about yourself, believe yourself.

**Technical coaching under load.** Video feedback is useful and it is not the same as a coach adjusting your position mid-rep. For learning to lift heavy, in person wins.

**Injury and rehabilitation.** Get assessed properly in person first.

**Complete beginners who feel lost in a gym.** The confidence problem is not solved by a better spreadsheet.

## Coaching or a plan? The quick test

| What you get | A plan | Real coaching |
|---|---|---|
| A programme | Yes | Yes |
| Adjusted when your week collapses | No | Yes |
| Feedback on what you actually did | No | Yes |
| Someone notices you went quiet | No | Yes |
| A direct line to the coach | No | Yes |
| Changes as you progress | Rarely | Yes |

If the right-hand column is not happening, you are paying coaching rates for the left-hand one.

## How to tell real coaching from a plan with a group chat

Five questions. Ask them before you pay.

1. **"What happens if I miss two weeks?"** A real coach adjusts the plan. A plan-seller tells you to pick it back up.
2. **"How do you use what I send back?"** If session feedback does not change next week's training, it is not being used.
3. **"How many people are in the group?"** There is no magic number, but a coach should know it and be able to say how they keep it manageable.
4. **"What do you do if my goal is different from the group's?"** The honest answer is either individual adjustment or "then this is not for you". Both are fine. Vagueness is not.
5. **"Can I speak to you directly?"** Not the group. You.

A coach who answers all five clearly is probably doing the work. One who deflects to features and app screenshots probably is not.

## What it costs, roughly

Published market rates in the UK put one-to-one personal training well above group and online formats per session, with online group typically the cheapest supervised option. Rates vary substantially by city and by coach experience.

## Honest limitations

Group formats mean compromise by definition. If your requirements are genuinely unusual, you will feel it.

The industry is also poorly regulated, and "online coach" covers everyone from an experienced practitioner to someone who bought a template pack last month. The five questions above are worth more than any qualification listed on a website.

And there is a version of this that works better than either: a short block of in-person sessions to learn the movements, then online coaching to do the volume. It is cheaper than sustained one-to-one and better than starting remote from zero.
