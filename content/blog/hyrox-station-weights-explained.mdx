The Hyrox standards are intentionally fixed. Same weights, same distance, same format, every event globally. That's the entire point of the brand: your time at one race is comparable to your time at every other. This is the current weight schedule by category.

## Doubles

Doubles uses open-category weights, split across two athletes. Each station has a split-and-tag structure:

- **Ski erg, rower:** One athlete works while the other rests in tag-style. 500 m each typical, but check event-specific rules.
- **Sled push, sled pull:** Both athletes push/pull together for the full distance.
- **Burpee broad jump, sandbag lunge:** Athletes can split the distance (50 m each on the lunge) or one does the full distance while the other rests.
- **Farmer's carry:** Same.
- **Wall ball:** Split into 50/50 reps.

The doubles strategy guide deserves its own article (see [Hyrox doubles handoff strategy](/blog/hyrox-doubles-handoff-strategy)). The short version: pair athletes of similar fitness and agree the split per station before the race.

## Masters categories

**The weights do not scale with age.** This is the single most common misconception about masters racing, and it is worth stating plainly: a 62-year-old in Open lifts exactly the same loads as a 24-year-old in Open. The 26/27 singles rulebook contains no age-based weight schedule of any kind.

What age groups actually do is determine your ranking. They run in five-year bands: Under 24 (16-24), then 25-29, 30-34, 35-39, 40-44, 45-49, 50-54, 55-59, and onwards in five-year increments to 85-89. Your band is set by your age on the day of your race, not at the start of the season.

Two age rules that do exist:

- Athletes may compete in the **Pro divisions up to and including the 80+ age groups** at regular HYROX races, with official age group rankings, podiums and flags applying as normal.
- For **World Championship qualification, athletes aged 60 and above qualify via the Open division only**, and compete at the World Championships using Open weights, repetitions and distances.

## Burpee broad jump rules

There is **no minimum jump distance**. This is widely misreported, including in earlier versions of this page. The rulebook sets no per-jump length, so a short jump is legal, it is simply slow. What the rulebook actually governs is form:

- **Chest to the ground** on every burpee. The rulebook defines this as the nipple line making clear contact.
- **Both feet take off and land together.** A maximum stagger of 5 cm is permitted between the front of one foot and the front of the other, on both take-off and landing. Anything greater is not permitted.
- **No additional steps or shuffles** at any point.
- **Hands no more than 30 cm forward of your toes** when starting each subsequent burpee, measured from the base of the palms. Once your hands are down they cannot move forward.
- You **may use a knee** coming out of the bottom of the burpee position.

The practical consequence is the opposite of what the myth implies. You are not penalised for short jumps, so if your jumps are collapsing under fatigue, shorten them and keep the rhythm rather than risking a staggered landing.

## Wall ball depth + height

The wall ball requires:
- **Squat to parallel.** Hip crease below the knee at the bottom of the squat.
- **Ball touches the target wall.** The ball must clearly hit at or above the target line.

Failed reps are called by the judge. Most wall-ball penalties come from athletes who get tired and start half-squatting in the last 30 reps.

## The honest summary

The Hyrox weights are fixed. Train against the exact race-day weight for your category from week 6 onward. Don't train sled push at 100 kg if you'll race 152 kg. The body adapts to what you load it with; load it with the truth.

The sled weights are the most-feared in training but the most-trainable. The wall ball is the most-deciding station. Train the wall ball at race weight, race-target height, in race-tired states.

The standards exist to make your time honest. Train against them.
