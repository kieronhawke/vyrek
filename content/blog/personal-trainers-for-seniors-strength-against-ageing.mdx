The most common mistake in training older adults is being too careful. Gentle circuits, light weights and a lot of stretching feel appropriate and do very little for the thing that actually determines independence, which is strength.

A good coach after sixty programmes progressive strength work, balance, and getting up off the floor. Not a softer version of general fitness.

## Why trust this

This page sticks to what is well established and says so where evidence is thinner. Nothing here is a substitute for medical advice, and there is a clear signpost below for when to get cleared first.

## What actually matters

**Progressive strength.** Muscle mass and strength decline with age, and that decline is substantially trainable. The stimulus has to be meaningful: sets that get genuinely hard, with load that increases over time. Light weights for high reps do not produce it.

**Balance and single-leg work.** Falls are the outcome that changes lives, and single-leg strength and balance work directly address the mechanism.

**Getting up and down from the floor.** A specific, trainable skill and one of the more meaningful markers of independence.

**Carrying.** Loaded carries build grip, trunk and the ability to move a suitcase, a grandchild or the shopping.

**Enough protein.** Older adults generally need more protein per kilogram than younger adults to achieve the same muscle response, and most under-eat it.

| Quality | Why it matters | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Progressive strength | The main driver of independence | Load that increases over time |
| Balance / single-leg | The mechanism behind falls | Split squats, step-ups, stands |
| Floor transfers | Getting up unaided | Practised as a skill |
| Loaded carries | Grip, trunk, real-world tasks | Suitcase and farmer carries |
| Protein intake | Muscle response to training | Higher per kg than younger adults |

## What a good coach will do

- **Ask about your medical history properly**, and insist on clearance where it is needed.
- **Test where you actually are** rather than assuming frailty.
- **Load the movements**, progressively, with technique that suits your joints rather than a textbook.
- **Programme balance work** as a real component, not a warm-up afterthought.
- **Train getting up from the floor**, unglamorous and valuable.
- **Adjust around arthritis, replacements and old injuries** without using them as a reason to do nothing.

## What a poor one does

Endless seated machines. Resistance bands as the entire programme. Circuits that never get harder. Treating every client over sixty as identical because of a number.

If your programme has not changed in three months, it has stopped being training.

## Questions to ask before booking

1. **"What is your experience with people my age and my history?"** Specifics, not reassurance.
2. **"How will you progress the load?"** If there is no answer, there is no plan.
3. **"How do you handle my [replacement/arthritis/blood pressure]?"** A good coach will have a considered answer or will say they want to speak to your GP or physio.
4. **"What will we test, and when will we retest?"** Progress you cannot see is progress you will stop paying for.
5. **"What happens on the days I feel awful?"** Everyone has them. The answer should be adjustment, not cancellation.

## When to get cleared first

Speak to your GP before starting if you have a cardiac history, uncontrolled high blood pressure, a recent surgery or injury, dizziness or balance problems with an unclear cause, or any condition where exercise has previously been restricted.

This is not a formality. It is the difference between a coach programming around something and programming into it.

## The myths worth ignoring

**"Lifting is dangerous at my age."** Poorly supervised lifting is risky at any age. Supervised progressive strength work is one of the better-evidenced things you can do for later-life health.

**"I should stick to walking."** Walking is excellent and it does not build strength. Do both.

**"It is too late."** Strength and muscle respond to training well into later life. Later means more careful, not futile.

**"I need to lose weight first."** Strength training while losing weight protects the muscle you would otherwise lose. Doing it in sequence is worse than doing it together.

## Honest limitations

This is general information, not a programme, and definitely not medical advice. Individual conditions change the answer substantially and that is exactly why supervision is worth paying for at this stage of life.

Coach quality also varies enormously in this area, and confidence is not competence. The questions above will tell you more than a website will.
