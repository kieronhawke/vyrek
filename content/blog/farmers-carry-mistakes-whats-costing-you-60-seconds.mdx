The farmers carry looks like the simplest station in HYROX and produces one of the most avoidable penalties. It is 200m carrying two kettlebells, at position six, with grip that station three already spent.

## Why trust this

Loads and the station standard are read from the official 26/27 singles rulebook, including the marked-box rule that carries a flat 30-second penalty.

## The standard, exactly

200 metres. Loads are 2 x 16kg Women's Open, 2 x 24kg Men's Open and Women's Pro, 2 x 32kg Men's Pro.

- **Depending on the venue, 200m may involve multiple laps.** It is your responsibility to know the athlete map and complete the required number.
- The station **begins and ends with removing and returning the kettlebells** from the marked box next to the start/finish line.
- You must **carry both kettlebells at all times while moving**.
- Failing to return equipment to the correct marked area is a **30-second penalty**, though correcting it before you exit the Roxzone avoids that.

## The five mistakes

**1. Training grip fresh.** The most expensive fault and it happens weeks before race day. In the race your grip arrives at station six already taxed by the sled pull at station three. If every carry in training is done fresh, you have trained a different exercise.

*The fix:* always do carries immediately after heavy pulling. Rope pulls, heavy rows, lat work, then straight into the carry.

**2. Miscounting the laps.** The venue decides whether 200m is one lap or several. Athletes assume, get it wrong, and either do extra distance or come up short.

*The fix:* read the athlete map before your wave. This is explicitly stated as the racer's responsibility.

**3. Setting the bells down mid-carry.** The rule is that both are carried at all times while you move. Putting them down for a rest is not the tactical option people think it is.

*The fix:* if you are close to failing, slow your pace rather than stopping. A slow carry beats a stopped one.

**4. Fumbling the return.** The station ends by returning the bells to the marked box. Tired athletes drop them near the box rather than in it, and that is a 30-second penalty.

*The fix:* place them deliberately. Two seconds of care against thirty seconds of penalty is not a close call. And if you get it wrong, fix it before you leave the Roxzone.

**5. Death-gripping from the first step.** Squeezing maximally at metre one guarantees failure at metre 150. Grip endurance is about the minimum tension that keeps the bells secure.

*The fix:* hold firmly, not maximally. Relax the shoulders, keep the bells still, and let your skeleton do more of the work.

## The bit nobody trains

Bell swing. A carry where the kettlebells swing costs energy and destabilises your trunk. Keeping them quiet is a skill, and it is trained by carrying, not by gripping harder.

Walk tall, ribs down, and let the arms hang rather than actively bracing them against your legs.

## The faults at a glance

| Fault | Costs you | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Training grip fresh | The station, in the race | Carries straight after pulling |
| Miscounting laps | Extra distance or a short station | Read the athlete map before your wave |
| Setting the bells down | The rule requires carrying while moving | Slow the pace, do not stop |
| Fumbling the return | 30-second penalty | Place deliberately in the marked box |
| Death-gripping from step one | Failure by metre 150 | Firm, not maximal |

## The practical part: three sessions

1. **The coupling.** 4 rounds: heavy hand-over-hand rope pull or 10 heavy rows, straight into 100m carry at race weight. This is the race's actual pattern.
2. **Grip endurance.** 5 x 60-second holds at race weight or heavier, 90 seconds rest. Plus dead hangs twice a week.
3. **Compromised distance.** 3 x (1km run, then 200m carry at race weight). Learn what the full distance feels like with your heart rate up.

## Honest limitations

Bell handle thickness varies between venues and manufacturers, and thicker handles genuinely are harder to hold. Train on whatever you have and accept some variance on the day.

Grip strength improves relatively slowly, so if it is your limiter, start earlier in the block than feels necessary.

Chalk availability varies by event. Do not build a plan that depends on it.
