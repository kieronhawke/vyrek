The Elite 15 is HYROX's professional tier: fifteen athletes per division, invited off a rolling 365-day points ranking. To accrue those points at all you need a **HYROX Athlete Licence**, applied for *before* you race, and results achieved while unlicensed never count.

That licence requirement is the part most people miss, and it is the reason some very fast athletes are not in the rankings.

## Why trust this

Ben Sutherland races Pro Doubles on the Elite 15 start list with his brother Harry. Their record includes a doubles best of 49:24 at the Warsaw Major in 2026, sixth at the World Championships in Chicago in 2025, and wins at Glasgow, Mechelen and Berlin. The qualification mechanics below are read from the official 26/27 rulebook rather than described second-hand.

## The licence comes first

Only athletes holding a valid HYROX Athlete Licence are eligible to accrue qualification points, referred to as NPI points. The rulebook is specific about what it does:

- **Mandatory** for Elite 15 qualification pathways.
- Must be **applied for before competing** in any race for those points to be eligible.
- Provides eligibility for earning points, selection consideration for Elite 15 races, and access to designated Elite start waves where applicable.
- Confirms agreement to HYROX sporting regulations, integrity policies and professional standards.

Two consequences worth being blunt about:

**You can race without one.** Nothing stops you entering Open or Pro unlicensed. Your results simply do not accrue qualification points.

**It cannot be applied retrospectively.** A licence bought after a strong race does not validate that race. If you think you might chase this pathway, the licence has to come first, and applications go through elites.hyrox.com.

## How the ranking works

Rankings run on a **rolling 365-day basis** rather than a fixed season, and points come from your best results inside that window:

| Format | Results that count |
|---|---|
| Singles | Best **5** results |
| Doubles | Best **3** results |

Invitations to the Elite Racing and Challenger Divisions are issued off those rankings.

The rolling window changes how a season is planned. There is no single cut-off date to peak for, and a strong result stays live for a year before ageing out. It also means consistency beats one spectacular day: five good races out-score one brilliant race and four mediocre ones.

## Not all races are worth the same

Points are awarded by **race tier**, reflecting the competitive depth of each field, across three eligible divisions in singles and doubles:

1. Age Group (AG) Pro Race
2. Elite Racing Series
3. Elite World Championship

Each tier carries a distinct maximum points allocation, rising with the strength of the field. So where you race matters as much as how fast you race, and a mid-pack finish in a deep field can out-score a win in a shallow one.

## The percentile model

Points use a percentile performance model comparing your finish time to the **winning time**, with fixed scoring for the leading placements and percentile scoring below that.

The Challenger Division table shows the shape clearly:

| Placing | Points | Time requirement |
|---|---|---|
| 1st | 108 | n/a |
| 2nd | 106 | n/a |
| 3rd | 103 | n/a |
| 4th | 100 | Within 10% of 1st place time |
| 5th | 1st time / 5th time × 100 | Within 12.5% |
| 6th | 1st time / 6th time × 100 | Within 15% |
| 7th | 1st time / 7th time × 100 | Within 17.5% |
| 8th-10th | 1st time / your time × 100 | Within 20% |

The gating percentages are the interesting part. Finishing eighth is worth nothing if you are more than 20% behind the winner. Placing alone does not score; you have to be genuinely close to the front of a real field.

## What this means if you are not an elite athlete

Almost certainly: nothing, and that is fine.

The Elite 15 is the top of a pyramid whose base is people trying to break 90 minutes for the first time. The age group Pro race is where competitive amateur racing actually happens, and it carries its own rankings, podiums and flags.

Where it does matter to ordinary racers is understanding what you are watching. When you see Elite 15 times, you are looking at athletes with licences, structured season plans across a rolling window, and race selection chosen partly for points. It is a different sport from an Open entry on a Saturday, and comparing your time to theirs is not a useful exercise.

## Honest limitations

This covers the singles rulebook's qualification system. **Doubles has its own specific qualification rules** (section 6.2.8) which I have not read in full, so treat the best-3 figure as the headline and check the doubles rulebook for the detail if you are chasing it.

The full points-per-tier table, strength-of-field weighting and the secondary tie-break procedure are all in the rulebook and are more detail than this page carries.

The 26/27 season is also described in the rulebook as involving transitional recognition arrangements for an inaugural season, so the system is new enough that specifics may be revised. Check the current rulebook before planning a campaign around it.
