Starting from nothing is a different problem from starting from unfit. Nothing means no routine, no gym confidence, no idea what a normal session looks like, and usually a few false starts behind you.

The advice that works here is almost embarrassingly modest, and that is the point.

## The first rule: you are training attendance

For the first eight weeks your goal is not fitness. It is turning up. Everything that follows depends on the habit existing, and almost every failed attempt fails because someone started at week-twelve intensity in week one, hurt, and stopped.

If you complete eight weeks of modest training, you have succeeded. Whatever else happened is a bonus.

## What the first eight weeks look like

**Three sessions a week.** Not five. Three is enough to progress and few enough to survive a bad week.

**Thirty minutes each.** Including getting changed. Short enough that "no time" stops being a reason.

**An effort you could repeat tomorrow.** If you cannot walk properly the next day, you went too hard. Soreness is not the goal and it is not the measure.

A workable shape:

| Session | What | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 30 min brisk walk or easy jog | Builds the aerobic base everything sits on |
| 2 | Full-body strength, 30 min | Squat, hinge, push, pull, carry |
| 3 | 30 min walk, jog, bike or swim | Whatever you will actually do |

That is it. It looks too easy. It is supposed to.

## The strength session, specifically

Five movements, two or three sets each, a weight that makes the last two reps hard but not ugly.

1. **Squat pattern**: goblet squat, or sit-to-stand from a chair.
2. **Hinge**: Romanian deadlift, or hip thrust.
3. **Push**: press-up on the floor or against a bench, or a dumbbell press.
4. **Pull**: a row of any kind, or a band pull.
5. **Carry**: walk 20m holding something heavy in each hand.

If you do only that, twice a week, for a year, you will be dramatically stronger than you are now. The programme is not the hard part.

## What to ignore

**Anything promising results in two weeks.** The timeline is real and it is longer than that.

**Supplements, at this stage.** Nothing in a tub matters until the training and the sleep are consistent.

**Fasted cardio, ice baths, tracking apps, macro splits.** All arguments about the last five per cent, being had by people who have not done the first ninety-five.

**Other people's programmes.** The routine your friend follows is built on their history and their week.

**The scales, mostly.** Weight moves for a dozen reasons that have nothing to do with fat. Weekly, same conditions, or not at all.

## What actually moves the needle

**Sleep.** More effective than anything else on this page and the most commonly ignored.

**Protein.** Most people starting out eat too little. It supports the muscle you are trying to build and it keeps you fuller.

**Walking.** Unglamorous, and the single biggest lever on total daily energy expenditure for most people.

**Consistency across months, not intensity across weeks.** The person who trains three times a week for a year beats the person who trains six times a week for a month, every time, and it is not close.

## When you will want to quit

Around week three. The novelty has gone, the results have not arrived, and the sessions feel like admin.

That is the normal shape of it, not a sign you have failed. The people who get fit are not the ones who felt motivated at week three; they are the ones who went anyway and reduced the session rather than skipping it.

**Have a minimum version.** A ten-minute walk still counts. On the worst days, do the minimum version rather than nothing, because the streak is what you are protecting.

## Honest limitations

This is general guidance, not a personalised programme. If you have a medical condition, are on medication that affects heart rate or blood pressure, are pregnant, or have an injury, get cleared by your GP first. That is not a formality.

Progress is also genuinely slower for some people than others, for reasons including age, sleep, stress and starting point, none of which are moral failings.

And there is a limit to what an article can do. If you have started and stopped several times, the missing ingredient is usually accountability rather than information.
