HYROX runs **wave starts at regular scheduled intervals**, commonly every ten minutes. You are assigned an official start time before race day, it is confirmed again at check-in, and you must have it clearly visible on your arm.

The rule worth knowing before anything else: starting in a different wave from your assigned one, without organiser approval, is a **disqualification**.

## Why trust this

Every rule below is read from the official HYROX singles rulebook for the 26/27 season, not from event FAQs or forum posts.

## How waves are organised

**By division.** Racers start alongside others in the same division, so Women's Open, Men's Open, Women's Pro and Men's Pro go off in their own waves rather than mixed. The Elite Racing Series is a separate start wave entirely.

**At intervals.** The rulebook's own example is every ten minutes, though the exact spacing is set per event depending on the size of the field and the hall.

**Communicated twice.** Your official start time is sent before race day and confirmed at check-in. If those two ever disagree, sort it out at check-in rather than on the start line.

## The three rules that carry real consequences

| Rule | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Starting in an earlier or later wave than assigned, without organiser approval | **Disqualification** |
| Not wearing the issued timing chip on an ankle at all times | **DNS** on the leaderboard |
| Waiting in the start tunnel once your wave has begun | up to **1 minute** penalty |

**The wave rule is absolute.** It is not a matter of being waved through by a marshal who does not mind. If you need a different wave, because of travel, childcare, or racing alongside someone, that is a conversation with the race organisers in advance, and they can approve it. Turning up to an earlier wave because you are ready is a DQ.

**The chip rule catches people out.** The chip goes on your **ankle**, and it stays there for the whole race. Failing to wear it correctly produces a Did Not Start result on the leaderboard even if you crossed the line, and there is no retrospective fix for a time that was never recorded.

**The start tunnel rule is about flow.** Once your wave has begun you are required to move forward with it. Hanging back in the tunnel to let the crush clear can cost you up to a minute.

## What your wave actually affects

**Congestion.** A busy wave means queuing at stations, and that time is in your total. This is one of the reasons comparing your finish to a friend's from a different event, or even a different wave, is unreliable.

**Who you race near.** Since waves are division-based, the people around you are running the same loads. That is useful pacing information if you use it and a trap if you chase someone fitter.

**Nothing about the course.** Same eight stations, same order, same weights. Your wave does not change the race, only the traffic.

## The practical part: race morning

1. **Know your start time before you travel.** It is sent in advance. Do not arrive planning to find out.
2. **Write it on your arm.** The rulebook requires it visible, and marshals do check.
3. **Confirm at check-in.** This is the moment to catch a discrepancy, not later.
4. **Chip on the ankle, and check it twice.** Before the warm-up and again before you enter the tunnel.
5. **Arrive earlier than feels necessary.** Large venues eat time between the entrance and the start area, and a missed wave is not something you can simply join late.
6. **Walk the Roxzone if you can.** Know which arch is IN and which is OUT, because going through the wrong one is a two-minute penalty.

Work out where your fitness sits before race morning rather than on it. The [fitness assessment quiz](/quiz) takes a few minutes.

## Honest limitations

Wave times themselves are set by each event once entries close, so nobody can tell you yours in advance of that. Interval spacing also varies by event, and the ten-minute figure is the rulebook's example rather than a guarantee.

Doubles and relay have their own rulebooks and their own start arrangements, so do not assume the singles rules transfer directly.

Rules are revised between seasons. This reflects 26/27; check the rulebook for the season you are racing.
