Most HYROX penalties follow one escalation: a **formal warning**, then **15 seconds** for a second infringement, then a **further 15 seconds** for every one after, with no more warnings. A handful of specific mistakes sit outside that ladder and cost considerably more.

Knowing which is which is worth real time, because the expensive mistakes are the avoidable ones.

## Why trust this

Every figure below is read from the official HYROX singles rulebook for the 26/27 season. Ben Sutherland races Pro Doubles on the Elite 15 start list, where penalties decide places.

## The standard escalation

This applies at the SkiErg, both sleds, burpee broad jumps, the row and the farmers carry:

1. **First infringement:** formal warning.
2. **Second infringement:** 15-second penalty.
3. **Every subsequent infringement:** an additional 15 seconds. No further warnings.

The practical read is that one mistake is free and the second is not. Judges are not looking to catch you out, but they are consistent, and a technique that drifts under fatigue will get called repeatedly.

## The penalties that cost more

| Infringement | Cost |
|---|---|
| Missing a run lap | **3-7 minutes per lap**, venue-dependent |
| Entering the Roxzone via the OUT arch, or leaving via the IN arch | **2 minutes** each time |
| Failing to return equipment to its marked area | **30 seconds** |
| Sandbag placed on the ground, barricades or feet during lunges | **15 seconds** each time |
| Waiting in the start tunnel | up to **1 minute** |
| Completing a station with the wrong weight and not repeating it | **Disqualification** |

**Missing a run lap is the most expensive mistake in the sport.** The rulebook's own worked example: a racer finishes in 1:24, missed two laps at a venue where the penalty is five minutes, and their official result becomes 1:34. Ten minutes, for a counting error.

Count your laps. Every lap, every run. It sounds patronising until you are on run six with your heart rate at 180 and genuinely cannot remember whether that was the second or third.

**The Roxzone arches are the other silly one.** Two minutes for going in the out door. Walk the transition area before your wave and know which arch is which.

**The wrong-weight rule is the only routine route to a DQ.** If you complete all or part of a station on the wrong load, you are required to repeat the entire station at the correct weight. Failing to do that is disqualification, not a penalty. A disqualified racer gets no results data, is excluded from every ranking and award for that race, and cannot continue competing in it.

Check the weight before you start each station. It takes two seconds.

## What counts as a no-rep

The rulebook's definition is broader than most people assume. Any execution that **deviates from the movement standard, or is unusual or uncommon, or results in a time advantage** will not be recognised, and becomes either an invalid rep or a time penalty.

That last clause is the one to understand. It is not a list of banned techniques. If a movement gains you time by not meeting the standard, it does not count, whether or not anyone anticipated it.

The two standards most commonly missed under fatigue:

**Wall balls.** The squat depth and the target contact both have to be there on every rep. Failed reps get called, and the pattern is always the same: athletes start half-squatting in the last thirty when it hurts most.

**Burpee broad jumps.** Chest to the ground, defined as the nipple line making clear contact. Both feet taking off and landing together, with a maximum 5cm stagger between the front of one foot and the front of the other. No additional steps or shuffles. Hands no more than 30cm forward of your toes when starting each subsequent burpee, and once your hands are down they cannot move forward. You may use a knee coming out of the bottom.

Note what is *not* in there: there is **no minimum jump distance**. Short jumps are legal, they are simply slow. So if your jumps are collapsing under fatigue, shorten them and keep the landings clean rather than risking a stagger call.

## The practical part: five things that cost nothing

1. **Count your laps out loud.** Every single one. This is the cheapest insurance in the race.
2. **Walk the Roxzone before your wave** and identify the IN and OUT arches.
3. **Check the weight at every station** before your first rep, not after.
4. **Return equipment to its marked area.** Kettlebells, sandbag, sled handles. Thirty seconds, and the rulebook notes that correcting it before you exit the Roxzone avoids the penalty.
5. **Keep the standard when you are tired,** particularly on wall balls. A no-rep costs more than the rep would have.

## Honest limitations

This covers the singles rulebook. **Doubles and relay have their own rulebooks** with their own penalty structures, and I have not read those, so do not assume these numbers transfer.

Penalties also vary in one specific way by venue: the missed-lap penalty is a range of 3 to 7 minutes, set locally rather than globally. Check your event's briefing.

Rules are revised between seasons. This reflects 26/27. Check the current rulebook before your race rather than trusting any article, this one included.

And a note on judging: many penalties are automatically detected by the timing and tracking systems rather than judged by eye, so the assumption that a marginal rep will go unnoticed is a poor one.
