HYROX Madrid runs **17 to 21 March 2027**. The race is the same eight stations in the same order at the same weights as every other HYROX, which means what you are really planning is not a race, it is a trip that happens to end in one.

That reframing is the useful part, because the trip is where travelling athletes lose time, not the course.

## Why trust this

The date is read from HYROX's own event listing rather than estimated from previous seasons. Confirm on the [official event page](/hyrox/events/hyrox-madrid) before booking, because 2027 dates this far out are the most likely to move.

## The course has not changed

Worth saying plainly, because "race guide" pages for international events often imply otherwise. Station order, distances and your division's loads are **fixed by the rulebook and identical everywhere**: SkiErg, [sled push](/hyrox/stations/sled-push), [sled pull](/hyrox/stations/sled-pull), burpee broad jumps, row, farmers carry, sandbag lunges, wall balls, each after a 1km run.

What varies by venue is the same short list as anywhere: track layout and laps per kilometre, floor surface (which changes sled friction noticeably), Roxzone length, and wave congestion.

So the honest version of a travelling race guide is mostly about travel.

## What actually changes when you fly to a race

**Your taper collides with your travel.** The last week before a race is when you cut volume and protect freshness. It is also when you are packing, flying, sleeping somewhere unfamiliar and eating differently. Those two things fight, and the travel usually wins.

**Sleep is the real cost.** A single bad night before a race is survivable. Three of them, in a strange bed, after a flight, is a measurable performance drag. Book somewhere quiet in preference to somewhere central.

**Food gets less controlled.** You will not have your usual breakfast. Decide in advance what you will eat on race morning and whether you are bringing it with you, because hunting for porridge in an unfamiliar city at 6am is not a plan.

**Kit becomes a single point of failure.** Trainers, race kit and anything you rely on goes in hand luggage. Hold bags get delayed and you cannot buy your way out of it the night before.

## The practical part: planning the trip

1. **Fly in at least the day before.** Two days if the flight is early or connecting. A delayed flight is a wasted entry and a wasted training block.
2. **Book accommodation for quiet, not for location.** You are there to sleep, not to sightsee before the race.
3. **Hand luggage: trainers, race kit, any nutrition you depend on.** Everything else can be replaced.
4. **Plan race-morning breakfast before you fly.** Bring it if it is not reliably available.
5. **Do the sightseeing after.** Walking a city for six hours the day before a race is a training error dressed up as a holiday.
6. **Build the trip into the plan in week one**, not week eleven. Logistics stress in race week costs more than a missed session.

## Should you travel to race at all?

An honest answer: for a first HYROX, probably not.

A home race removes flights, unfamiliar beds, different food and currency at exactly the point when you have plenty of other variables. The [UK and Ireland calendar](/blog/hyrox-uk-calendar-2026) has six races across the season and one of them is likely closer.

Travel makes sense when you have raced before and know what your body does on race morning, or when the trip is the point and the race is the excuse. Both are good reasons. "It looked cheap" is not.

## Honest limitations

This page carries the date and the venue from the official listing and no more. Wave times, hall layout and entry prices are set by the event and published closer to it.

I have not raced Madrid, and this is deliberately not a guide to the city, the venue's quirks or the local scene. Anything claiming detailed course knowledge of a venue nobody on the team has raced is guessing, and guessing is what got the previous version of this site's calendar page every date wrong.

2027 dates are also the most likely to be revised. Confirm before booking flights.
