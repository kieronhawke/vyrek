The row is the one HYROX station where the fastest split is often the wrong answer. It sits at position five, straight after burpee broad jumps have spiked your heart rate, and it is the only place in the race where you can legally sit still and breathe.

These sessions build the stroke, and more importantly the discipline to use the row as what it is.

## Why trust this

The movement standard is read from the official 26/27 singles rulebook. Two provisions worth knowing: you **may stop rowing at any time**, rack the handle and rest with your feet on or off the plates, but you may **not stand up or leave the seat** until the 1,000m is complete and a judge confirms it.

Most athletes do not know the first one.

## 1. Sequencing at low rate

- **Work:** 5 x 300m at an easy pace with the stroke rate deliberately capped low, 60 seconds rest.
- **Focus:** legs, then hips, then arms on the drive. Reverse it coming back. One count out, two counts back.
- **Cue:** push the floor away before anything else moves. If your arms bend first, reset.

## 2. Controlled 1,000s

- **Work:** 3 x 1,000m at your target race split, 2 minutes rest.
- **Rule:** hit the split, do not beat it. Going faster is failing the session.
- **Why:** you are building the ability to hold a number under adrenaline, which is the actual race skill.

## 3. Recovery rowing

The session that matches the race's design.

- **Work:** 5 rounds of: 15 burpee broad jumps, then 500m row at a deliberately controlled split.
- **Goal:** your heart rate should come **down** during the row.
- **What it teaches:** that a controlled row after a spike is a tactical asset, not lost time.

## 4. Aerobic base

Longer, steadier work that underpins everything.

- **Work:** 4 x 2,000m at a conversational pace, 2 minutes rest. Or 30 minutes continuous.
- **Why:** the row rewards aerobic capacity more than technique once your sequencing is sound, and that capacity carries into the running too.

## 5. The full sequence

- **Work:** 3 rounds of: 20m burpee broad jumps, 1,000m row, 1km run at goal pace.
- **What it teaches:** how the row sits between the hardest heart-rate spike and a kilometre you still have to run properly.

## How to know it is working

| Test | Do it | Progress looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Fresh 1,000m | Monthly | Faster at the same stroke rate |
| 3 x 1,000m holds (2) | Fortnightly | Same split, shorter rest |
| Recovery row (3) | Monthly | Heart rate dropping during the row |
| Full sequence (5) | Monthly | Run pace holding after the row |

Row two and row four are the ones that predict a better race. A faster fresh 1,000m on its own does not.

## What tends to fail first

Sequencing, then the recovery ratio. Tired athletes start opening the arms early and rushing back up the slide, which raises the rate, drops the power per stroke, and removes the only breathing space the stroke contains.

If your split is drifting and your rate is climbing, slow the recovery down rather than pulling harder.

## Putting it into a week

| Day | Session |
|---|---|
| Mon | Aerobic base (4) |
| Tue | Threshold run |
| Wed | Rest or sequencing (1) |
| Thu | Recovery rowing (3), or the full sequence (5) later in the block |
| Fri | Strength |
| Sat | Long easy run |
| Sun | Rest |

The row doubles as aerobic work, so it is one of the few stations you can train often without an impact cost.

## Honest limitations

Rowing is genuinely coachable and thirty minutes with someone watching you is worth more than any article, including this one.

Damper setting is preference rather than rule. Chasing an "optimal" number matters far less than sequencing and pacing.
